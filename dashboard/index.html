The problem

The same token, two prices, and a race measured in seconds.

Liquidity is split across chains. Every big trade on one chain leaves its twin on another chain mispriced. Today that gap is captured by private bot farms, and everyone else just eats the bad price.

≠

Fragmented prices

A whale sells on Base and the price drops 15%. On Unichain the same asset still trades at the old price. Users on either side get a worse deal until someone closes the gap.

⚙

Off-chain bots everywhere

Arbitrage today means servers polling two RPCs, private keys on a box, and retry logic. It's opaque, fragile, and closed to almost everyone.

7s

Too slow until now

On ~7-second reactive blocks the gap was gone before a reaction could land. Reactive Omni's ~1-second blocks with instant finality finally make an on-chain arbitrage loop viable.

How it works

One reactive contract. Four steps. About two seconds.

Every step happens on-chain and leaves a transaction you can click. The only thing between the price gap and the trade is Reactive Network.

01
observe

Mirror both markets

Each pool emits the standard Uniswap V2 Sync event on every trade. The reactive brain on Lasna subscribes to both and keeps an exact copy of the reserves on each chain.

02
decide

Size the trade on-chain

When the gap is wider than the two 0.3% swap fees, it computes the profit-maximising size in closed form: two pools collapse into one virtual pool, and the optimum has an exact formula.

03
act

Two callbacks, one tx

It fires a buy on the cheap chain and a sell on the expensive chain from the same reactive transaction. Each leg carries its own slippage floor and timestamp deadline.

04
settle

Close the loop

The executors report back with LegExecuted events. The brain books realised PnL, releases its lock and immediately re-checks the gap, chaining another arb if one is still there.

Why Reactive Omni

Fast enough that the gap never gets old.

Arbitrage is a race against other traders and against the price drifting back. Omni's CometBFT consensus gives ~1 s blocks with instant finality, so the reaction lands while the opportunity is still there.

Parallax also follows the buildathon rule for Base's 200 ms blocks: every deadline and cooldown uses timestamps, never block counts.

Under the hood

Built like it will hold real money.

The reactive contract does the real work, and every leg is guarded on the destination chain at the moment it executes.

🔒 In-flight lock

The brain never double-fires on the Sync events its own trades create. The lock expires by timestamp if a leg never reports back.

🧯 Circuit breaker

Two partial fills in a row pause the engine automatically. Directional risk stays bounded until a human reviews it.

📉 Live re-quote

Executors re-price against the pool before swapping. If the price moved past the floor, the leg is skipped and reported, never forced.

⏱ Timestamp deadlines

Legs expire after a fixed number of seconds and cooldowns are time-based, which is safe on Base's 200 ms blocks.

🔁 Replay protection

Each arb ID executes at most once per executor. Only the Reactive callback proxy, acting for this exact brain, can call in.

🧪 29 passing tests

The full loop is simulated in Foundry: sizing, caps, chained arbs, lock expiry, partial fills, the breaker, access control and a fuzzed optimality check.

FAQ

Questions people ask.

Is this real money?

No. Everything runs on testnets (Unichain Sepolia, Base Sepolia and Reactive Lasna) with test tokens called pWETH and pUSD. The mechanics are the same as they would be on mainnet.

What is Reactive Network?

Reactive Network lets a contract subscribe to events on other chains and react to them automatically. When a subscribed event fires, the network calls the contract's react() function, and the contract can request transactions ("callbacks") on any supported chain. No off-chain bot is needed.

Why doesn't it bridge tokens between the chains?

Bridging is far too slow for arbitrage. Like real cross-chain trading desks, each executor holds inventory of both assets on its own chain. The buy and the sell happen in parallel, so no bridge sits in the hot path.

What happens if one leg fails?

The executor emits LegSkipped with a reason (expired, price moved, no inventory…). The brain records a partial fill, releases its lock and allows one retry. A second partial fill in a row trips the circuit breaker and pauses the engine.

How is the trade size chosen?

Two constant-product pools chained together act like a single virtual pool. Parallax computes that pool's reserves and uses the exact closed-form optimum, dx* = (√(γ·e0·e1) − e0) / γ. That gives the profit-maximising trade with no loops, capped by a configurable risk limit.

Can I try it myself?

Yes. Open the dashboard, connect MetaMask on Base Sepolia or Unichain Sepolia, and use the whale panel to dump or pump one pool. The test tokens mint for free. Then watch Parallax spot the gap and close it, live.

Does it work with real Uniswap pools?

The brain listens for the standard Uniswap V2 Sync(uint112,uint112) event, so it can mirror any V2-style pair. To trade against one, you'd point the executors at a router instead of the demo pool.

Who pays for gas?

The reactive contract holds lREACT, which pays for its executions on Lasna. Each executor holds a little native ETH, which pays for its callbacks. Both can be topped up at any time, and a coverDebt() function settles anything owed.
